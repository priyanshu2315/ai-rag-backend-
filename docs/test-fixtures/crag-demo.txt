SECTION 1 - DELIVERY CONFLICT ESCALATION

This procedure applies when a delivery-related conflict threatens a commitment made to a client. The delivery associate first records the conflict and attempts coordination with dispatch. If that does not resolve it, the associate escalates to the Delivery Lead, which is Tier 1. If the Delivery Lead cannot resolve the conflict within two business hours of accepting it, the Delivery Lead escalates to the Head of Operations, which is Tier 2. Tier 2 must resolve the conflict within a maximum of eight business hours from Tier 2 acceptance. This is a resolution deadline, not merely an acknowledgement deadline.

The incident record must include the client commitment, shipment reference, owner, acceptance time and next action. Internal coordination continues while the incident is open. Notification requirements are recorded separately in the stakeholder communication policy.

SECTION 2 - INTERNAL STOCK DISAGREEMENTS

A disagreement about stock placement that does not affect a client commitment is handled by the Inventory Lead. The Inventory Lead informs the Warehouse Manager after the internal investigation. The normal resolution target is three business days. This procedure does not apply to a delivery conflict that threatens a client commitment. Its reporting rules and deadline must not be substituted for the delivery conflict procedure.

SECTION 8 - CLIENT COMMITMENT COMMUNICATION POLICY

For any delivery-related conflict affecting a client commitment, the Client Account Owner must be informed immediately when the risk is identified. This obligation applies even if the delivery team is still trying to resolve the conflict internally and even if no formal escalation has occurred. Internal handling is not a reason to delay informing the Client Account Owner. The Delivery Lead and Head of Operations remain the formal escalation owners described in the delivery conflict procedure.

For a routine internal stock disagreement with no impact on a client commitment, the Inventory Lead follows the separate stock procedure. Do not apply that procedure's delayed reporting rule to a threatened client commitment. The incident record should show when the Client Account Owner was informed.

SECTION 16 - REPORTING DEFINITIONS

For this reference pack, calendar year 2023 runs from 1 January through 31 December 2023. An annual operating cost is the full calendar-year cost of running a warehouse. It is distinct from revenue, profit, client invoice value and city expenditure. A named site manager is responsible for the warehouse; a named escalation owner is responsible for a particular incident tier. These roles must not be treated as interchangeable.

The delivery conflict procedure measures its Tier 2 resolution deadline from Tier 2 acceptance. It does not describe a guaranteed total duration from the original detection of the conflict. All business-hour deadlines in this pack must be reported as business hours rather than converted into calendar hours.
